The innovation

One hub that makes wireless networks quantum-safe and self-defending

Today, encryption and intrusion detection are usually separate products, built for servers and data centers rather than small wireless devices, and almost all of them still depend on cryptography that quantum computers are expected to break. QuantumSecure brings three layers of protection together in a single hub designed for the wireless and IoT edge.

Quantum-safe key exchange

Every device agrees on its session keys with ML-KEM-1024, a lattice-based algorithm standardized by NIST. Unlike RSA and elliptic-curve methods, it is designed to resist attacks from quantum computers, so traffic recorded today stays private in the future.

  • Unified. Encryption, authentication and AI threat detection work together instead of as separate tools.
  • Built for the edge. Designed around real wireless and IoT devices and tested on a live network.
  • Standards-based. Uses the NIST post-quantum standards that governments are now adopting.
  • Quantum-ready. Designed to grow with quantum computing rather than be broken by it.

Why it matters

Wireless traffic captured today can be decrypted tomorrow

Most IoT and wireless links still rely on RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography, which large quantum computers are expected to break. Devices deployed now often stay in the field for a decade or more, so they need quantum-safe protection from day one.

  • 🕵️

    Harvest now, decrypt later

    Attackers can record encrypted traffic today and store it until quantum computers can unlock it.

  • 📡

    Constrained devices, long lifetimes

    Sensors, gateways and controllers are hard to update once deployed, so weak cryptography lingers for years.

  • ⚠️

    Encryption alone is not enough

    Spoofed devices, injected packets and unusual traffic patterns need to be detected as they happen, not after the damage.

The risk window

When is it already too late?

Security experts use a simple test known as Mosca's theorem. If the time your data must stay secret, plus the time it takes to upgrade your devices, is longer than the time until quantum computers can break today's encryption, your data is already at risk.
